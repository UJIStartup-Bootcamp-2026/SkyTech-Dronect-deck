# Pitch Deck Method — Design, Language, and Editorial Reference

Version: 0.2 · 6 November 2026  
Context: UJIStartup Bootcamp 2026  
Status: reusable editorial and visual method. MCP/server implementation is deferred.

Revision focus: turn the visual and linguistic character of the reference into actionable guidance while preserving distinct identities and compositions. Technical integration notes are retained in an appendix for later use.

## Purpose

Give a team working with different AI applications a common, versioned method for explaining a venture concisely, checking its claims, and preparing a pitch deck. Preserve freedom over voice, brand, language, and slide composition.

Use this document directly as a shared reference for people and AI collaborators. Its core is a common standard of clarity, evidence, and visual hierarchy. Each project can develop its own visual identity and language within that standard. A future MCP can distribute the method and run explicit checks; current use requires no server.

## Critical interpretation

The source is an investor-oriented teaching example. Its example prices, customer counts, funding amounts, growth rates, and timelines are illustrative; none is evidence about the team's project.

The principles take precedence over incidental layouts. For example, a 24-month roadmap, three product functions, a six-block mechanism, monochrome styling, and a contact QR can be useful defaults without becoming universal constraints. A static deck should communicate without requiring a video to play; a live demo may still be appropriate when the event format permits it.

Some final examples retain generic or inconsistent material: the value slide still depicts a loss despite its benefit-oriented title, and the closing slide retains the generic slogan criticized in the cover example. The implementation should check the resulting message instead of assuming that every final example is a complete template.

## Design and language contract

The reference's recognizable character comes from direct assertions, concrete mechanisms, visible evidence, restrained typography, generous space, and selective emphasis. Operationalize those relationships rather than reproducing a particular slide arrangement.

The numerical budgets and production settings below are proposed working defaults, not measurements or requirements published by Senda. Adjust them after inspecting the rendered result and the actual presentation conditions.

| Level | What belongs here | How to apply it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stable principles | Meaningful headline, clear reading order, defensible claims, legible evidence, separation of present results and future intent | Preserve these throughout the deck. Changing a claim's truth status is never a stylistic adaptation. |
| Adaptable defaults | Copy budgets, number of supporting items, light background, a restrained accent, static product sequence, near-term roadmap | Start here; revise when the audience, content, accessibility, or presentation format justifies it. Record the reason for a substantial departure. |
| Creative choices | Brand colors, typeface, imagery, icon style, composition, metaphor, and emphasis on an appropriate emotional moment | Choose deliberately for the project. Keep the choices coherent within its deck. |

Recognizability should survive a change of brand, layout, or language. If two unrelated projects receive the same sequence of layouts, the same generic headlines, and interchangeable graphics, revise the work around each project's actual evidence and mechanism.

## Language system

### Headlines communicate the takeaway

Use an optional small section label to orient the reader. Let the main headline state the useful conclusion, benefit, mechanism, or decision. A label such as “Business model” can sit above a headline that explains who pays and for what.

- Aim for one short sentence, normally about 6–14 words in English or Spanish. Clarity and necessary qualifications outrank the count.
- Use the headline's strongest concrete noun and verb early. Prefer the customer's action or outcome to a description of the slide.
- Make numeric headlines carry a unit and a meaningful period or baseline. Specify the population or scope when it changes interpretation.
- Match the strength of the statement to its evidence. A target headline must visibly read as a target; a laboratory result must not read as a production guarantee.
- Avoid a headline that could appear unchanged in almost any startup's deck. On the closing slide, a compact identity or request may replace an assertion.

### Tone is direct, calm, and commercially understandable

Use active verbs, familiar terms, and short clauses. Retain necessary technical terms when they explain the mechanism or differentiation, and explain them briefly on first use. Spell out the business meaning of an acronym when the audience needs it; do not spend the main slide merely defining acronyms.

Remove inflated adjectives, absolute promises, and filler such as “revolutionary”, “seamless”, “unparalleled”, “all-in-one”, “cutting-edge”, “líder indiscutible”, or “transformamos el futuro” unless a specific, substantiated meaning makes the term useful. These are editorial warning signals, not a banned-word algorithm.

Prefer a named actor, an action, and a result. When the information is uncertain, use an explicit status such as “we estimate”, “our target”, “planned pilot”, or “internal test”. Preserve that status during translation and compression. Warmth and personality are welcome when they illuminate the customer or project; credibility does not require a cold voice.

### Compression preserves the argument

1. Write the full intended meaning and identify its evidence.
2. Choose the one idea the audience must retain from this slide.
3. Keep the conclusion and the minimum support needed to understand it on screen.
4. Move definitions, calculations, limitations, and secondary detail to notes or an appendix, except qualifications essential to interpreting the visible claim.
5. Check that the shorter version still answers who, what changes, and why the audience should believe it.

Use roughly 25–45 visible words for a simple live-pitch slide as a starting point, excluding compact source credits. Tables and mechanism diagrams may need more labels; a standalone reading deck may need more explanation. Density is a review warning, not an instruction to reduce the font size or omit evidence.

Keep wording parallel in lists and comparison rows. Use consistent terms for the same customer, feature, and metric throughout the deck. Display one number format and currency convention per language. Distinguish percentage changes from percentage-point changes and always identify a comparison baseline.

### Original rewriting examples

The following are invented teaching examples. Every product capability, figure, test, and request here is hypothetical and unrelated to Dronect or any other actual team project.

| Generic or weak wording | More useful wording | What improves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Una plataforma integral para revolucionar la gestión” | “Agrupamos pedidos y avisamos de los retrasos.” | Names actions the listener can understand and inspect. |
| “Resultados extraordinarios: un 40% mejor” | “En 5 pruebas internas, el tiempo medio bajó de 10 a 6 minutos.” | Identifies the measure, baseline, and limited evidence scope. |
| “Nuestra IA autónoma resuelve las incidencias” | “El prototipo clasifica incidencias; un técnico valida la respuesta.” | Preserves the actual boundary of the capability. |
| “Modelo de negocio escalable y recurrente” | “Cada tienda paga una cuota mensual por local.” | Explains the payment mechanism without asserting unproven economics. |
| “Gran interés del mercado” | “Buscamos 2 oficinas para un piloto de 4 semanas.” | Makes the next requested action explicit without claiming traction. |
| “Una mejora radical para todos” | “Objetivo del piloto: reducir el tiempo de preparar cada visita.” | Frames an unvalidated benefit as an objective with a specific task. |

Apply the same reasoning in English and other languages rather than translating sentence patterns mechanically. The hypothetical result above could become “Across five internal tests, average task time fell from 10 to 6 minutes”; it must retain the test scope.

## Visual system

### A stable hierarchy, with room for composition

Give the reader a clear entry point, supporting evidence, and a final detail level. A useful default is: small section label, prominent takeaway, one dominant visual or evidence group, and a compact source line. A quiet logo can provide continuity. Covers and closings can use a different hierarchy appropriate to their purpose.

Use alignment and spacing to reveal relationships. Reserve heavy outlines, filled shapes, bold figures, or an accent color for the claim, component, or comparison that deserves attention. Secondary material should remain readable while attracting less attention. The reference's faint grid and repeated logo are optional presentation devices, not requirements.

For a standard 16:9 widescreen slide, these are practical starting points to verify after rendering:

| Element | Starting treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Outer margins | Approximately 5–7% of the slide width; align repeated elements consistently. |
| Headline | Around 30–40 pt on a 13.33 × 7.5 inch slide; allow a short second line when needed. |
| Main copy | Around 20–26 pt; avoid long paragraphs. |
| Hero number | Around 44–64 pt when a single metric drives the slide; place its unit and label nearby. |
| Table and diagram labels | Usually 16–20 pt; simplify or split content when labels become hard to read. |
| Compact source credit | Around 12–14 pt, with a fuller citation in the notes when needed; essential qualifications belong with the claim. |
| Typography | One clear sans-serif family by default, with a small, consistent set of weights. A suitable brand typeface may replace it. |
| Palette | Neutral foundation with a restrained brand accent. A dark profile can work when hierarchy and contrast remain clear. |

Inspect at the expected presentation size and viewing distance. Point sizes alone do not establish legibility. Keep color-coded distinctions understandable through labels, shapes, line styles, or position as well.

### Choose the visual by the argument

| Narrative job | Suitable composition | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establish the size of a problem or result | Large metric with context and a short explanatory line | Include the relevant baseline, population, period, and source; avoid a decorative figure with no definition. |
| Make the product tangible | Real screenshot, photograph, or clearly labelled prototype with a few callouts | Show the relevant operation. Label concept images and planned functions. |
| Explain a causal relationship | Compact cause–mechanism–outcome arrangement | Make direction explicit and distinguish a demonstrated relationship from a hypothesis. |
| Explain how something works | A short user sequence or a simple system diagram | Start with about 3–6 meaningful steps or components. Mark proprietary and external elements where relevant. |
| Explain differentiation | Compact comparison table, grouped by alternative type when useful | Use criteria that matter to the customer; preserve unknowns and competitors' strengths. |
| Demonstrate change over time | Legible line or column chart suited to the metric | Identify axes, period, and units. Distinguish actual and projected data. Use an honest scale. |
| Explain execution | A short phase table connecting milestones, measures, and resources | Keep future outcomes visibly provisional and funding status explicit. |
| Establish execution ability | Concise team cards or a capability map | Select relevant experience and responsibilities; avoid a wall of biographies or advisor logos. |

Use imagery, icons, arrows, and containers when they carry information. Keep icons in one visual family and pair unfamiliar ones with words. A product image, customer context, or distinctive illustration can become a project's visual signature when it helps understanding. Avoid generic hero imagery, repeated decorative cards, arbitrary gradients, and automatic icon grids that displace evidence.

### Cohesion without cloned decks

Each project should choose a small set of visual commitments: palette, typeface, spacing rhythm, heading treatment, chart conventions, imagery style, and source placement. Reuse those commitments while selecting each slide's composition for its content.

Variation should follow a change in the argument. A metric slide, a product view, and a competitor comparison naturally need different structures. Repeating a structure is appropriate for a deliberate comparison; forcing a different layout on every slide is unnecessary.

The same hypothetical result can support two distinct treatments:

- **Product-led treatment:** a screenshot shows the task, two callouts identify the changed steps, and a nearby metric summarizes the test result.
- **Evidence-led treatment:** a paired-column chart compares the measured task times, with a small product crop explaining the intervention.

Both must retain the test scope, baseline, unit, source, and qualification. The available evidence and the audience's question determine the choice. A project can express its identity through color and imagery in either treatment.

## Editorial and visual review loop

Review both the slide and its role in the whole deck. Classify findings as **must fix**, **should improve**, or **optional direction**, and explain the effect on the audience instead of assigning a pseudo-objective quality score.

| Review lens | Question | Typical correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Takeaway | Can a brief glance identify what this slide establishes? | Replace the topic-only headline with a supported assertion, or remove competing focal points. |
| Claim integrity | Does the visible wording preserve status, scope, units, and evidence? | Restore a qualification, attach its source, or mark the evidence gap. |
| Hierarchy | Is there an obvious reading order and a dominant information group? | Change placement, scale, contrast, or grouping before adding decoration. |
| Density | Can the audience understand the slide while listening? | Reduce competing ideas, split the slide, or move supporting detail to notes. |
| Visual reasoning | Does the graphic demonstrate a mechanism, comparison, or result? | Select a composition suited to the argument or remove a redundant visual. |
| Language | Are the actor, action, and meaning concrete? | Replace vague nouns, inflated claims, and unexplained jargon with specific language. |
| Identity | Does this feel like the same project without becoming repetitive? | Preserve visual commitments and vary composition according to the evidence. |
| Deck continuity | Do price, customer, product status, and the final request agree across slides? | Reconcile the narrative and canonical figures before polishing appearance. |

The brief-glance check is an editorial heuristic, not a validated timing requirement. Perform a separate close reading for accuracy and an oral run-through for pacing.

## Instructions for human and AI collaborators

Use the following workflow directly with the presentation contract and project evidence:

1. Identify the audience's decision, the venture's actual stage, and the strongest available evidence.
2. Draft the narrative and one intended takeaway per slide. Flag missing information rather than filling gaps with invented claims.
3. Choose a coherent project-specific visual direction. Explain how its hierarchy, restraint, and use of evidence relate to the reference.
4. Write visible copy and speaker notes separately. Preserve essential qualifications in the visible claim.
5. Select a composition for each slide based on its narrative job. For pivotal slides, consider two materially different compositions before choosing one; avoid generating cosmetic alternatives for every slide.
6. Run the editorial and visual review loop, then inspect the rendered deck when one exists.
7. Hand off the reasoning as well as the copy so the next collaborator can continue without reconstructing it.

Each slide handoff should include: `slide_id`, `purpose`, `takeaway`, `visible_copy`, `claim_ids`, `visual_brief`, `speaker_notes`, `open_questions`, and `review_findings`. In `visual_brief`, describe the composition, hierarchy, assets, evidence placement, and the reason for the choice. Shared terminology and method version travel with the deck; a collaborator can propose a justified change instead of mechanically preserving an earlier draft.

## Acceptance criteria for the current method

1. An unfamiliar collaborator can identify the intended language and visual character without being given a fixed master slide.
2. A proposed headline expresses the slide's actual takeaway and preserves evidence status.
3. Two distinct visual treatments can communicate the same evidence without losing meaning or recognizable editorial discipline.
4. Every material numeric claim keeps its unit, relevant period or baseline, and evidence reference.
5. Slides separate screen copy from oral explanation and supporting detail.
6. Design choices strengthen information hierarchy and remain readable in the intended viewing conditions.
7. The project has a coherent visual identity and uses composition appropriate to each narrative job.
8. Review findings distinguish required corrections from preferences and explain their audience impact.

## Evidence and narrative workflow

### 1. Establish the presentation contract

Record audience, intended decision, venture stage, language, live versus standalone format, available time, mandatory event requirements, and brand preferences. Ask only for information that changes the narrative or the validity of a claim.

A pitch seeking pilot customers needs a different final request from a fundraising deck. A pre-validation venture needs an honest validation plan rather than invented traction.

### 2. Maintain a claim register

Each material claim should have an identifier, statement, status, source reference, date or period, and owner. Numeric claims additionally need a value, unit, denominator where relevant, and calculation or measurement method.

Suggested statuses: `observed`, `reported`, `estimated`, `target`, `hypothesis`, and `unknown`.

- `observed` requires a supporting observation or measurement record.
- `reported` identifies the reporting person or source; it does not mean independently verified.
- `estimated` exposes assumptions and a calculation.
- `target` belongs to a future period and must be labelled accordingly.
- `hypothesis` remains a proposition to test.
- `unknown` creates a missing-information item rather than a fabricated figure.

The tool can detect missing metadata or arithmetic conflicts. It cannot establish the truth of an external source merely because a citation field is populated.

### 3. Build the narrative before rendering slides

For each proposed slide, record:

- Its purpose and the audience question it answers.
- One principal takeaway and its evidence references.
- The essential visible copy, separated from speaker notes and appendix detail.
- A visual brief explaining what the visual must demonstrate.
- Any unresolved question or dependency.

Conciseness must preserve qualifications that change meaning. Move supporting detail into notes or an appendix when possible; never shorten “pilot planned” into “pilot secured”.

### 4. Review in two complementary ways

**Mechanical checks** can validate required fields, configurable copy budgets, evidence references, numeric units, totals, dates, and cross-slide use of canonical figures. Examples include annual price versus monthly price, market arithmetic, funding allocation totals, and conflicting customer counts for the same period.

**Editorial review** evaluates clarity, relevance, credibility, causal connections, meaningful differentiation, and fit to the audience. The connected AI should explain its judgments and propose revisions. These judgments should not be represented as objective or investor-predictive scores.

### 5. Render and verify separately

Prepare a stable deck specification, readable Markdown, speaker notes, and a review report before choosing a rendering tool. PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, or another presentation tool can express the same method through different production workflows.

Rendered output needs a visual inspection for text overflow, contrast, hierarchy, chart readability, citations, and working links or QR destinations. Text validation alone cannot certify the appearance of a deck.

## Appendix — Deferred technical integration

The following notes preserve the earlier MCP proposal for future implementation. They are not prerequisites for applying the design and language method. No application inventory, hosting choice, SDK selection, or server development is needed for the present revision.

### MCP interface proposal

MCP supports resources, prompts, and tools. The proposed split follows the [official server concepts](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/learn/server-concepts) and [architecture overview](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/learn/architecture), checked on 6 November 2026. Implementation must select SDK and protocol support against the actual target clients before claiming compatibility.

| Interface | Proposed responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Resources | Versioned editorial method, section criteria, claim and slide schemas, and optional visual profiles. |
| Prompts | Guided workflows for intake, outlining, compression, slide critique, and complete-deck review. |
| Tools | Retrieve criteria, assemble contextual review instructions, validate structured decks, and export supported textual formats. |

Proposed tools, subject to client requirements:

| Name | Inputs | Output and execution boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `get_pitch_method` | Method version and optional section | Criteria and provenance; no generative-model call. |
| `prepare_pitch_workflow` | Presentation contract and chosen task | Contextual instructions and expected output schema for the connected AI. |
| `validate_pitch_deck` | Structured deck, claim register, profile | Deterministic findings with slide IDs, rule IDs, severity, and explanation. |
| `export_pitch_package` | Valid structured deck and format | Markdown or JSON content; no publication or external document edits. |

Writing and semantic critique can run in the connected AI without embedding a second model in the server. This avoids requiring a shared paid model account merely to distribute the method. If independent server-side AI review is desired later, it requires a separate provider, cost, and data-handling decision.

Because client support for resources and prompts must be verified, core method retrieval and workflow preparation should also be exposed through tools. An equivalent portable Markdown package gives collaborators another way to use the method when their application cannot connect to the server.

### Collaboration and state

Agree on one canonical project package: presentation contract, claims, deck specification, and method version. Exchange this package between contributors rather than relying on private chat memory.

A server distributing common rules does not automatically synchronize the team's deck. A first release can be stateless and operate on the package supplied in each request. A later shared workspace would need persistent storage, access control, revision history, and conflict handling. Updates should use an expected revision so one collaborator cannot silently overwrite another's work.

Local stdio suits locally launched clients. A remotely hosted Streamable HTTP service is a candidate for team access across devices, subject to each platform's supported connection modes. Hosting and access arrangements remain undecided.

### Future implementation acceptance criteria

1. Every inspected source section maps to an editable criterion with source attribution.
2. The same structured package can be consumed by at least two selected target clients, with supported capabilities documented.
3. An unsourced monetary claim produces an explicit evidence gap; the workflow does not fill it with an invented amount.
4. A hypothesis, planned pilot, or target cannot silently become a validated result during compression.
5. Cross-slide discrepancies in price, customer counts, funding, and time periods produce actionable findings.
6. Changing audience, duration, or brand does not require changing the method's code.
7. A narrative-only export preserves claim references, speaker notes, and unresolved items.
8. Proposed method changes carry a version so collaborators can identify which rules informed a draft.

### Decisions deferred until implementation

- Which exact applications and account tiers must connect, including any collaborator whose application has no MCP support?
- Is the first deliverable a content-and-review package, or must it also generate an editable deck in a specific presentation tool?
- Is the first use case the immediate bootcamp pitch, with its duration and intended ask, or a general reusable toolkit?

These choices determine deployment and adapters. They do not block the editorial and visual work described above.
